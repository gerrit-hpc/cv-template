# Calibration Bands

Heuristics for mapping company and process signals to the three calibration bands the interview-prep skill reads: `style`, `difficulty`, `tone`.

These are starting points, not laws. Override based on what the user has actually heard from the recruiter or experienced firsthand.

---

## Style — what kind of questions to expect

| Band | When it fits | Tells |
|---|---|---|
| `structured-behavioral` | Large public tech (FAANG / FAANG-adjacent), big consultancies, regulated industries. Rubric-driven scoring, leveled rubrics, calibrated bar. | "We use a structured interview process." Recruiter mentions "leveling" or "calibration". Multi-interviewer panels with rotating focus areas. |
| `unstructured-conversational` | Seed / Series A startups, founder-led companies, agencies. Conversation-first, less rubric. | Recruiter is the founder. Stages are "a chat with X". No assignment, no rubric. |
| `case-heavy` | Strategy / management consulting, some product roles, fintech. Hypotheticals, business problems, sometimes timed. | "Case interview" mentioned. Assignment is a strategy memo or business analysis. |
| `coding-heavy` | Big tech IC roles, some quant / infra roles. Multiple coding rounds, system design as primary signal. | 2+ rounds described as "coding" or "technical". HackerRank / CoderPad / pairing on real code. |
| `culture-heavy` | Mission-driven orgs, agencies, early-stage where culture fit dominates. Values questions, "why us", references to mission. | Recruiter dwells on values / mission. Stage names like "culture interview" or "values panel". |
| `mixed` | Series B–D scale-ups, most mid-sized tech. A blend of behavioral, coding, and culture across stages. | The most common band. Use this when no single signal dominates. |

---

## Difficulty — what bar to expect

| Band | When it fits | Tells |
|---|---|---|
| `junior-screen` | Role is junior / mid. Process is short (2–3 rounds), broad questions, no deep system design. | Role title is "Engineer" or "Senior Engineer". Recruiter says "let's chat" not "deep dive". |
| `mid-rigorous` | Senior IC at scale-up. 3–4 rounds, real technical depth but not staff-level. | Stack-specific questions expected. Some system design. |
| `staff-level-deep-dive` | Staff / Principal / Architect roles. 4–6 rounds, ambiguous system design, cross-functional scenarios, technical strategy questions. | Role explicitly says staff or principal. Recruiter warns "deep technical bar". Senior eng or director on the panel. |
| `leadership-fit` | EM / Director / VPE / Head-of-X roles. People stories dominate; technical depth secondary. | Role is people management or org leadership. Panel includes peer leads + skip-level. |
| `hybrid` | Player-coach roles (Tech Lead, Lead Engineer, Engineering Manager with IC duties). Both technical depth and leadership stories. | Role straddles. Equal weight to both. |

---

## Tone — how to phrase suggested answers

| Band | When it fits | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| `formal` | Enterprise, public companies, regulated industries, government, EU corporates. | Crisp STAR, full sentences, no slang. Address interviewer formally (de: Sie). |
| `casual` | Most mid-size tech, post-Series-A startups. | Professional but warm. Contractions OK. First names. |
| `startup-scrappy` | Seed / Series A, founder-led. Hustle is a virtue. | Show range, show willingness to do uncomfortable things. Mention scrappy moves explicitly. Speed and bias-to-action over polish. |
| `corporate` | Big consultancies, banks, traditional enterprises. | Stick to STAR rigidly. Quantify everything. Avoid colloquialisms. |

---

## Cross-band hints

- **Stage and company size**: FAANG technical → coding-heavy + staff-level-deep-dive + corporate. 5-person startup technical → unstructured-conversational + mid-rigorous + startup-scrappy.
- **Industry skew**: Fintech → expect compliance / regulatory questions even in technical rounds. Healthcare / biotech → expect mission and ethics questions in any stage. Crypto / web3 → expect philosophy questions about the space.
- **Recent funding event**: Just raised → expect questions about scaling and hiring. Just laid off → expect questions about runway, focus, what you'd cut.
- **Hiring manager seniority**: First-time manager → expect more behavioral, less calibration. Senior leader → expect calibrated rubric and harder follow-ups.

## Prompt bank — when signals are ambiguous

If after the web pass and the first user-interview pass you still can't pick a band confidently, ask one or two of these:

- *"What did the recruiter say about how the interview process works? Anything about how they evaluate candidates?"*
- *"Do you know if they use a structured rubric or it's more 'whoever they like'?"*
- *"Is the role explicitly staff/principal level, or is the bar more 'senior contributor'?"*
- *"Have you talked to anyone who's interviewed there? What did they say about the format?"*
- *"How formal does the company feel to you from the website and the recruiter calls — buttoned-up or scrappy?"*

Don't ask all of these. Pick the one or two that close the specific gap you have.

---

## Always defer to user-known facts

If the user has firsthand information that contradicts these heuristics, the firsthand information wins. Document the override in the Justification paragraph so future runs (or a refresh) preserve the reasoning.
